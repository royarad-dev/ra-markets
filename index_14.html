RA Markets · refreshed Saturday, August 15, 2026 · 5:00am ET for Roy Arad. Markets are shut for the weekend — every level below is Friday's close. Friday was a split tape, and the split is the story: the S&P 500 slipped 0.17% to 7,785.76 and the Nasdaq 0.28% to 26,729.16, while the Russell 2000 rose 0.51% to a record 3,068.42. Two data points did it. July retail sales fell 0.6% against +0.1% expected — the first decline in nine months and the largest in fourteen, with the control group at −0.4% versus +0.3% — and then the University of Michigan's August preliminary sentiment landed at 51.0 versus 54.5 expected, down from 55.2, with one-year inflation expectations rising to 4.3%. A consumer that has stopped spending while still expecting 4%-plus inflation is a stagflationary shape, and it finished off the September rate-hike trade: odds fell to roughly 29–31% from 35% on Thursday and about 67% at the end of July. Remember the 2026 debate is hike-versus-hold, not when to cut — the Fed held at 3.50–3.75% on Jul 29 with Hammack, Kashkari and Logan all dissenting for a hike, the most early dissents against a new chair (Kevin Warsh) since 1970. Small caps took that relief; mega-cap tech did not. Broadcom fell 5.94% to $392.99, erasing about $105B and acting as the single largest drag on both indices, after BofA published on XPV — Broadcom's off-balance-sheet vehicle for financing AI racks — estimating up to $370B of senior debt by mid-2029. Applied Materials beat and raised guidance and still fell ~5% on a flat gross margin. The 10-yr ended at 4.69%; reports differ on whether it finished up or down ~5bp, because the data-driven rally faded into the close as crude firmed — and this came a day after the $25B 30-year auction stopped at ~5.22%, the highest at auction since 2001. Crude rose ~5% on the week (WTI ~$82, Brent ~$88.5) after two ADNOC tankers were attacked in the Strait of Hormuz on Aug 13 and Treasury Secretary Bessent floated an indefinite naval blockade of Iran. Gold ~$4,373, bitcoin $62.8K, ether $1,878 — US spot bitcoin ETFs bled ~$385M on the week, giving back much of the prior week's $853M rebound. For the week: S&P +0.36% and Nasdaq +0.14%, a third straight weekly gain and the longest run since early April; Russell +1.11%; Dow −0.56%. Q2 season is 88% done with blended earnings growth of 50.4%. Ahead: Home Depot and Reddit's S&P 500 entry on Aug 18, Target plus the FOMC minutes Aug 19, Walmart Aug 20, then Nvidia and July PCE together on Aug 26 and Jackson Hole Aug 27–29 with Warsh's first keynote as chair.
